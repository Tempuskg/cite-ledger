How to check BibTeX references for corrections and expressions of concern

A free single-file check against Crossref, with a worked example of a correction and an expression of concern recorded after the works were published.

A work can change after you first cite it. Publishers issue corrections, and sometimes an expression of concern while questions about a work are looked into. Crossref's Crossmark documentation describes the gap this leaves: a reader "who downloaded a PDF version before the update would have no way to know that it has been updated" (Crossref, Crossmark documentation, last updated 2020-04-08). The same holds for an entry that has sat in a .bib file since the paper was first read.

Worked example

Take these two BibTeX entries for real Crossref works. The first has a correction recorded in Crossref; the second has an expression of concern. The entries carry only a title, journal, year and DOI.

@article{vaccine2016,
  title   = {Virus-Like Nanoparticle Vaccine Confers Protection against Toxoplasma gondii},
  journal = {PLOS ONE},
  year    = {2016},
  doi     = {10.1371/journal.pone.0161231}
}

@article{fos2013,
  title   = {Impairment of FOS mRNA Stabilization Following Translation Arrest in Granulocytes from Myelodysplastic Syndrome Patients},
  journal = {PLoS ONE},
  year    = {2013},
  doi     = {10.1371/journal.pone.0061107}
}

Cite Ledger 0.1.1 reports:

refs.bib:1 vaccine2016 [P2 correction] Correction recorded in Crossref for 10.1371/journal.pone.0161231 (2024-03-21): notice 10.1371/journal.pone.0301214. Recorded by the publisher.
refs.bib:8 fos2013 [P2 expression-of-concern] Expression of concern recorded in Crossref for 10.1371/journal.pone.0061107 (2023-01-11): notice 10.1371/journal.pone.0278805. Recorded by Retraction Watch and the publisher.
  • The correction on 10.1371/journal.pone.0161231 is the notice 10.1371/journal.pone.0301214, dated 2024-03-21 in the Crossref metadata, eight years after the work appeared. A correction may leave a work's findings intact, so read the notice to see what it changes and whether your citation relies on it.
  • The expression of concern on 10.1371/journal.pone.0061107 is the notice 10.1371/journal.pone.0278805, dated 2023-01-11. Crossref holds it twice, once from Retraction Watch and once from the publisher, and Cite Ledger reports both sources in one finding. An expression of concern is not a retraction: it tells readers a question has been raised. Read the notice before deciding how to cite the work.
  • Each finding links to the notice on doi.org and to the work's record on api.crossref.org, so you can check what Crossref holds.

A notice can be followed by another. Check the file again before you submit, not only when you first add an entry.

Steps (free tier)

  1. Install Cite Ledger from the VS Code Marketplace or Open VSX.
  2. Open your .bib file.
  3. Run Cite Ledger: Check References in This File from the Command Palette (or use the checklist button in the editor title bar).
  4. Open the Problems panel. For each correction or expression of concern, open the notice link, read what it says, and decide whether your text still cites the work correctly.

Limits

  • Does not check DOIs registered outside Crossref, for example DataCite.L1
  • Crossref's retraction data can miss some retractions.L2
  • 'Unverified' means no matching record was found, not that the reference is fabricated.L3

Cite Ledger reports the notices Crossref records. A notice the publisher has not deposited with Crossref, and Retraction Watch has not recorded, will not appear. More detail: limits.
